# Bodo culture: visual research (2026-11-18)

This research was done in the browser to ground the invitation's colours, patterns and story in real Bodo textiles and customs. Sources are listed at the end. Anything that will be printed should still be checked with someone from the community.

## 1. Colours: what real Bodo textiles look like

**The traditional trio is "Gwmw Gwthang Batho Gang":** yellow (*gwmw*), green (*gwthang*) and parrot-feather green (*bathogang*). Plain dokhona also come in red (*gwja*), violet (*solayrong*) and blue (*neel*).

**The bridal dokhona, "Dokhona Thawsi" (pure dokhona), is yellow.** In photos it's a clean **lemon to marigold yellow**, not the dull mustard used in round 1.

The colours are **bright and saturated**, often a solid ground with **one strong contrast colour** woven in:

| Pairing seen | Where |
|---|---|
| lemon yellow body + **scarlet** border, white diamond chains, gold motifs | designed dokhona (bridal style) |
| **saffron/marigold** dokhona + **emerald green** blouse/wrap + **scarlet** jwmgra | Bagurumba dancers: the most recognisable Bodo image |
| **black** + gold extra-weft | dokhona, jwmgra |
| **navy/royal blue** + gold | aronai |
| **magenta** + white, **emerald** + white, **pink** + white | aronai |
| lilac + magenta, pink + gold | dokhona |
| **green** with white stripe borders | gamsa (men's wrap, worn by the groom) |
| raw **eri silk** beige + indigo/purple borders | endisi shawl |
| marigold vest + parrot-green agor border | khot gosla (men's vest) |

## 2. Patterns: how agor is built

**Agor** (motif) is woven as **extra-weft**: fine thread floats on a solid ground. Up close everything is built from **tiny steps**: small diamonds, dots and short lines. Motifs are fine and repetitive, **not chunky blocks**, and are mostly **one contrast colour** on the ground. A designed dokhona adds a few small coloured accents (red or green dots in a gold motif).

A weaving song quoted by IIAD: weave "butterflies sucking nectar and birds flying in the sky".

### Anatomy of an aronai
Every one follows the same plan:
1. **Outer edge:** a narrow chain of small diamonds.
2. **Hajw agor:** tall **sawtooth triangles** along both long edges, pointing inward, each filled with fine fern-like hatching. *An aronai is only an aronai if it has the Hajw/Pahar agor on both edges.*
3. A thin double rule.
4. **Centre column:** large round or oval **medallions** made of concentric rings of dots and tiny diamonds with a flower at the centre (peacock's eye). They alternate with **paired peacocks or birds** facing each other, paisley-like sprigs and small diamonds.
5. **Ends:** plain ground with fine horizontal stripes, then a **fringe**.

The scarf is about 1.5 m × 0.25 m. It is given to honour guests and placed around their neck.

### Anatomy of a designed dokhona
- **Body:** solid colour, sometimes with **scattered small butis** (flowers, paisleys or diamonds, each with a tiny coloured centre).
- **A wide border panel at the hem** and a **vertical decorated column**: dense agor in the contrast colour, such as diamond chains, sawtooth bands and floral vines.
- **Tassels** at the corners.
- The types are *Mwdwm Gongse* (designed all over), *Jing Jing Aolo / Lophai* (border only) and *Gejwrasbwese* (light on the body and border).

The **jwmgra** (women's shawl) uses **larger** agor than the dokhona, and the *Hajw* (mountain) agor is the most popular on it.

## 3. Wedding and faith (for story and copy)
- **Marriage is "Haba".** The traditional ceremony is **Hathasuni Khurnai** (Hathasuni Haba):
  - The bride and groom sit **in front of the Bathou**, and the priest invokes **Bathou Bwrai** and declares them married.
  - The bride cooks **Ondlakhari** (chicken in rice powder, with no spice or turmeric) and offers a little to the household deities.
  - She then serves rice and curry on **banana leaf** in two wooden **hatha** bowls to the groom and the villagers.
- **The groom must wear a gamsa** (traditionally green with a white border). The bride wears a yellow **Dokhona Thawsi**.
- **Bathou** means "five principles": air, fire, earth, water and ether.
  - The **sijou** plant (*Euphorbia neriifolia*) embodies Bathou Bwrai.
  - It's fenced with **18 pairs of bamboo strips tied with five bands**. From the bottom, the bands stand for **birth, pain, death, marriage and peace**.
  - Worshippers face **east**, and the lamp is circled **five times**.
- **Bwisagu:** the spring new year festival.

**Ideas this gives the card:**
- The fourth band of the bamboo fence (marriage) can be the moment the opening "ties".
- A **banana leaf with the hatha** suits the reception or meal section.
- The **aronai** fits the RSVP: "we will honour you with an aronai".

## 4. Music and dance
- **Bagurumba**, the "butterfly dance", is performed only by women. They wear a saffron dokhona, a green blouse and a scarlet jwmgra held out like wings. The movements copy butterflies, birds, plants, river waves and wind.
- **Instruments:**
  - **kham:** a long wooden barrel drum, laced end to end with leather thongs in a zigzag
  - **sifung/siphung:** a long bamboo flute with five holes and a low tone
  - **serja:** a bowed string instrument with a round body and a scroll bent forward
  - **jotha:** cymbals
  - **gongwna:** a bamboo jaw harp
  - **tharkha:** a split-bamboo clapper
- The music is pentatonic (F major pentatonic).

## 5. What this changes in the design (round 1 → round 2)
1. **Colour.** Replace the dull mustard and olive with a **bright gwmw yellow** and **marigold** as the stars, **scarlet** borders and **emerald** for actions. Gold and white are for motif thread.
2. **Motifs.** Redraw the agor as **fine extra-weft**: small step-diamonds, dot rings and thin lines in **one contrast colour** on a solid ground. Use the real structures: the hajw sawtooth border, peacock-eye medallions and diamond chains. No chunky blocks.
3. **Aronai band.** Use the real anatomy: sawtooth hills on both edges, a medallion column, striped ends with a fringe.
4. **Doors.** Weave them as a **designed dokhona**: a solid ground with butis, a wide border panel and tassels at the hem. Gold-on-black or white-on-emerald would read richly; yellow with scarlet borders is the bridal option.
5. **Curtains.** Use a bright yellow dokhona body with a **scarlet hem panel** and a vertical agor column at the inner edge, with tasselled corners.
6. **Butterflies.** Draw them as scarlet jwmgra "wings" with agor edges, which links them directly to Bagurumba.
7. **Story.** Bring in the Bathou fence with five bands, Hathasuni Haba, the gamsa and Dokhona Thawsi, the aronai welcome, and the kham and siphung for the sound toggle.
